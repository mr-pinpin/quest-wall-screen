# The atlas's cost to gaming on the Air's media engine (2026-11-03, 13:28-13:40 UTC)

Setup: Air, 4 virtual displays 1920x1200 (pattern moving on 20 and 21, 22 and 23 still), atlas-vis test sender
(v1 layout, fits at 90%: 3456x2160), demand 20 pri 1, 21 pri .5, 22/23 pri .2, `"game":1` where noted.
Game stand-in: `bench/encprobe.swift`, HEVC 2560x1600, 60 fps, real-time, 80 Mbit/s, 15 s, whole frame changing.
Probe figures: encoded fps, encode latency p50 / p99 (submit -> output, ms).

Reading:
- A full-size atlas next to a 2560x1600 game encode overloads the Air's one media engine: the game encode falls to
  40 fps and its latency to ~280 ms (queueing), the atlas's own encode to ~120 ms. Per-screen frame rates
  (prifps) and skipping still screens (damage) do not fix it: the cost is the atlas's pixel size per encode.
- damage had no effect: ScreenCaptureKit delivered the still screens at the same rate as the moving ones, every
  frame with dirty rects (0% clean). Candidate for DROP unless a suite pair shows otherwise.
- gamecap (the atlas within 1920x1200 while a game plays) alone restores the game encode to 60 fps / 14 ms; with
  gameyield the game encode is at its no-atlas baseline, and the extra screens still update at ~17 fps.
- To be confirmed on the headset with the real game stream (bench/gaming_pairs.sh pairs 5 and 6, gate + verdict).
